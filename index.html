About Me

I am an MSc Astrophysics student at Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich (LMU), working on cosmology, early universe physics, and transient astrophysical phenomena. My research combines theoretical modeling, numerical simulations, and observational data analysis to study gamma-ray bursts, supernovae, and the evolution of cosmic structures.

I am actively involved in observational follow-up programs at Wendelstein Observatory and contribute to NASA GCN Circulars for transient astrophysical events.

Life Beyond Research

I have been obsessed with astrophysics and science since I was a kid. I always wanted to become a scientist like the ones I saw in cartoons such as Cloudy with a Chance of Meatballs. That curiosity never really left me, and it slowly became the path I followed into physics and cosmology.

Music was my first passion. I started playing the violin as a child, later learned the viola, and performed in orchestras for several years. Rehearsals and concerts became a normal part of my life, and I loved the discipline of working together to create something larger than any individual musician could. Eventually, as university became more demanding, I made the difficult decision to leave orchestras behind and devote my time to studying physics. I still play occasionally, although now it's simply for myself.

Sports came next. I started skating as a kid, then moved into inline hockey, and later ice hockey. I played as a defense player for my team, trained a lot, and competed in different national leagues. I won two bronze medals — one in inline hockey and one in ice hockey. I also became a skating and hockey coach, coaching beginners and kids. Watching my students gain confidence on the ice was just as rewarding as competing myself.

Unfortunately, after a serious injury, I had to stop doing intensive sports. It wasn't an easy chapter to close, but it changed my life in a way where I had to slow down and focus more on other things like studying and research. The determination that once pushed me through long training sessions now finds its place in research, coding, and tackling difficult scientific problems.

Languages are another passion. I'm currently studying German, Turkish, and Arabic at the same time — not because it's the easiest thing to do, but because I enjoy challenging myself and discovering new ways of understanding people and cultures. Sometimes I wonder whether trying to learn three languages at once is a good idea, but I'm still running the experiment.

And when I'm not studying or working, I enjoy hiking with my dog.

My Dog

My dog came into my life when I needed him the most. Since then, he has been with me through almost every stage of my life.

We went through everything together — from preparing for college entrance exams in Iran, getting accepted into one of the best universities there, and studying through my bachelor's degree. He was there during long study days, stressful moments, and all the changes that came with university life.

Later, he even moved with me to Germany so I could continue my master's degree. Moving countries was a big change for both of us, but having him there made everything feel more stable and familiar.

He has been my biggest academic partner in his own way. He doesn't understand cosmology or physics, but he has been present through all of it — every paper, every deadline, every milestone.

He is my biggest motivation, and I love him very much. I want to make him proud one day, so that when he meets other dogs at the park and they are all sniffing around, he can somehow tell them that my mama is a scientist.
